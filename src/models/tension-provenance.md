## Chosen reformer variant and explicit mismatch

This draft retains the Zola basic phase's tabletop legs and ball between ankles.
It **differs from the related catalog's feet-supported, props-none plan**. The
related ID `original-reformer-supine-arm-press` is comparison only. It is not
mapped, approved or automatically eligible. The visible SVG labels the different
ball variation as well as its unknown ball type/size.

The source does not establish the ball's type, dimensions, pressure, load or
material. The generic sphere's unitless radius and the two medial ankle contact
points are independently authored drawing choices. No mini-ball or gym-ball
classification is inferred. The sphere stays round; compression and squeeze
forces are not modeled. Both legs retain an authored rigid tabletop arrangement;
exact hip/knee/ankle positions and foot orientation are not measured source data.

The arms start above the shoulders and move through an independently authored
down/up arc with a small constant elbow bend. Near-straight elbow geometry is a
drawing choice, not an explicit instruction from the retained basic transcript.
Head support, head-down posture, torso support thickness, carriage/headrest
positions and precise body clearance are authored assumptions. Shoulder-block
clearance is not measured or modeled. The later curl/leg extension is absent.

Each hand stays at its handle endpoint. Each authored rope route is
`hand → fixed ideal point pulley → carriage attachment`. The two straight free
segments have a conserved total geometric length. A bounded scalar root solve
determines carriage translation. The carriage, body, head/back/pelvis support
anchors, legs and ball translate together; the arms move relative to them.
Pulley locations, ideal point approximation, return segment and moving attachment
layout are original schematic choices. No finite pulley-wrap length, rope
elasticity, friction, spring or force behavior is modeled. This length-conserving
construction is not a claim that the source measured or validated that motion,
nor a manufacturer-specific rope routing or setup.

## Chosen Cadillac variant and independently authored path

The selected preparation uses bent knees with feet supported on the table.
Table/frame, head/back/pelvis supports and both feet remain fixed. Both hands
hold spring-handle endpoints. Fixed behind/above anchors connect to the handles
through original mathematical coil polylines. The visual endpoint length changes
with hand motion; no physical spring behavior is inferred from it.

The source establishes bent elbows at preparation start but does not establish
the full elbow or hand endpoints. This draft maintains a constant bent-elbow
linkage and authors the full hand arc, outward elbow plane and return. It does
not claim those are an observed or approved preparatory trajectory. No Hundred,
chest curl, head lift, leg lift or later straight-elbow routine is combined with
the selected phase. Table padding, body/foot contact coordinates, head support,
attachment height, handles, coil count, amplitude, dimensions, range and timing
are drawing choices. `original-cadillac-arm-spring-press` is comparison only;
the complete draft remains unmapped and unreviewed.

## Geometry and review boundary

Every number is unitless and authored: dimensions, angles, path range, support
offsets, five phase boundaries, easing, spring drawing and projection. World x
is head→feet, y is width (anatomical left positive), z is height, floor z=0.
Both arms move bilaterally; the `activeSide` input is retained as metadata only.
Mirroring changes only projected horizontal coordinates about SVG width 640.
Neither side selection nor mirroring changes anatomy, contacts or world poses.
Start/end worlds and equipment match exactly. The parent owns paused defaults,
preview duration, reduced motion, resets and view controls.

**Forces, resistance, spring settings, stiffness, pretension, compression,
pressure, loading, friction, inertia, manufacturer dimensions, actual hardware
clearance, setup procedures and safety conditions are not modeled.** No spring
or equipment setting is recommended. These are kinematic diagrams, not dynamics
simulations or validation of exercise suitability or equipment safety.

Mechanical tests cover finite poses, fixed limb segments, handles, supported
feet, fixed/moving support frames, rope conservation, fixed spring anchors,
changing visual spring length, bilateral symmetry, side metadata, mirroring,
five phases, continuity and exact closure. DOM and server-rendering tests check
SVG metadata, source-context labels, unique accessible references, finite output
and absence of copied media. Parent visual/browser review remains separate.
